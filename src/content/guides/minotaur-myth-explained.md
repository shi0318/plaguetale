Resonance does not retell the Minotaur myth. It starts **after** it. That is the single most useful thing to know going in, and it comes straight from Asobo's own words in **DEVBLOG #3, "Into The Myth"** — a post the studio itself flags as containing minor spoilers.

## The myth, in the order it happens

Asobo's own summary runs like this, and it is worth having straight before you play:

1. **Minos**, king of Crete, breaks a promise to **Poseidon**.
2. The sea god's revenge lands on Minos's queen, **Pasiphaë**, who is cursed to give birth to the **Minotaur** — half man, half bull.
3. To hide the child, Minos commissions the master architect **Daedalus** to build a labyrinth no one can escape.
4. Minos then demands a toll from Athens: **fourteen youths every year**, sent in to feed a beast that has grown savage.
5. **Theseus**, son of the Athenian king, volunteers.
6. **Ariadne**, Minos's clever daughter, gives him a thread. He goes deep, kills the Minotaur, and follows the thread back out.
7. Then vanity takes over. He **abandons Ariadne**, and she curses him to a life of solitude and misfortune.

## Where the game begins

Asobo's line is precise enough to quote: **"Resonance begins the moment the thread vanishes, and nothing guarantees the way back…"**

So the labyrinth is not a puzzle box with a solution already written into legend. The lifeline is gone before your part of the story starts. That reframes the pursuit hook from the store page — the presence that follows Sophia and seems to know her every move — as something you are inside of without an exit, rather than something you can out-plan.

## Theseus is the second hero

The Steam description talks about "the entwined destinies of two heroes linked by fate" without naming the second one. The devblog does: **Sophia follows Theseus's path on her journey across the island**, and the studio calls him the character in whom their central themes are "especially present."

The pairing is deliberately built as a mirror:

- **Sophia** was born into struggle and violence, and is searching for something better than that.
- **Theseus** is a well-off figure who **turns to** violence to get what he wants.

Asobo's own summary: both have "the same — forgive the pun — bull-headed resolve and desire to control their own destiny." The recurring motifs they name are **savagery**, and **taming what will not be tamed**.

## What the myth is doing thematically

The Plague Tale series has always run on loss of innocence, illness and devotion. Asobo describe Resonance as continuing those and adding its own: at its core they call it **a game about family, loyalty and betrayal**, and "a story about dualities."

The stated reason for reaching for this particular myth is that it gave them a way to explore their characters' **vulnerabilities and the loss of control they are forced into**. Read that next to what Sophia's performer says about playing her "overcome with rage / out of control" in [our voice-actor page](/sophia-voice-actor/), and the two halves line up.

## Do you need to know the myth to follow it?

No. Asobo are explicit that Resonance is a **self-contained experience for newcomers**, with "plenty of surprises for the fans of the series." Knowing the legend is not a prerequisite — but knowing that Theseus was cursed for betraying the woman who saved him is the kind of detail that changes how a scene reads.

## Confirmed vs not confirmed

- **Confirmed:** the myth is the game's structural setting; Theseus's path is the one Sophia follows; the game opens after Ariadne's thread is gone; themes are family, loyalty and betrayal.
- **Not confirmed:** whether Ariadne, Minos, Pasiphaë or Daedalus appear as characters rather than as history, and how the Minoan-era sections are structured. Daedalus is only confirmed as the figure who wove light into the island.
