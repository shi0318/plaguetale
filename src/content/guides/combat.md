Combat in **Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy** is the biggest break from the earlier games. The Steam description calls it dynamic and violent melee built around agility, tricks, sharp reflexes, parries, and powerful strikes. You play Sophia, described as skilled, agile, and strong-willed, and the enemies chasing the island's treasure are meant to be fought, not only avoided.

## What the store page confirms

Straight from the official Steam text:

- Sophia engages in **dynamic and violent melee combat**.
- Combat centres on **agility, tricks, and sharp reflexes**.
- You **perfect your parries** and **unleash powerful strikes** to survive deadly encounters.
- Many enemies pursue you to the island to reclaim a mysterious treasure, so you have to **stay one step ahead**.
- Against the creature behind the myth you **can't stop it — only hide, outsmart, and push forward**.

Steam also lists adjustable difficulty and a way to play without timed input. Pick the setting that lets you read enemy movement; the [difficulty and accessibility guide](/difficulty-accessibility/) covers what those options are.

## How combat differs from Innocence and Requiem

The earlier games leaned on stealth, a sling, alchemical ammunition, and fire and light used against rat swarms. Resonance foregrounds direct melee instead, and there are no rat swarms to kite. Players who finished it describe the combat change as the main reason it feels unlike *Innocence*, and launch reviews after **27 August 2026** talk about exciting combat rather than hiding. Stealth still exists in places, but walking into a fight is not a fail state. The [stealth or combat guide](/stealth-or-combat/) explains when hiding is actually required.

## Parry basics: the attack colour rule

Walkthrough coverage of the boss fights describes three kinds of attack, each with its own answer:

| Attack | Tell | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Normal swing | No glow | Parry — fills the stun bar |
| Yellow attack | Yellow flash | Parry for a counter; **Echo's Return** can throw the attacker |
| Red attack | Red flash | **Dodge**, unless you bought the Aegean Deflect mastery that lets you parry red |

The practical habit is the same in every fight: watch the wind-up, parry the swing you can see, then decide whether a powerful strike is safe. A guard with a weapon trail is a parry, not a stealth reset. Missing the parry twice means healing and resetting your spacing. The full skill list, including Aegean Deflect, is in the [skills unlock order](/plague-tale-legacy-skills-unlock-order/), and the boss-by-boss mix of colours is on the [all bosses page](/plague-tale-legacy-all-bosses/).

## How to parry a flanking guard

Tight streets and carnival alleys are built for two bodies, not one duel. The first guard is the parry you can see; the second is the one who hits you while you are busy.

1. Finish or abandon the first parry. A half-finished riposte with a sword in your back is how the attempt ends.
2. Turn until the second guard is in front of the camera, then parry.
3. Step so neither guard is behind you before you punish.
4. Only after both are down, deal with the light gate or the side room.

A flank is not a failed stealth attempt. The street was a fight from the start. Dying here reloads the encounter; it does not refund a skill point — see [what death resets](/story-select-cleanup/).

## Weapon first, light and throwables second

Sophia's default kit is a sword and off-hand defence for parrying; the [weapons page](/weapons/) covers what she carries and the Blades you collect. Two other tools sit beside it:

- **Throwables.** The Fated Cast skill lets her store a picked-up throwable on her belt and use it later, and the achievement list includes killing an Archer with a throwing knife (*Returning the favor*).
- **The Minoan sphere.** It manipulates light. In the early chapters it opens light gates and paths; walkthrough coverage says it becomes an offensive tool only after Light Concentrate in Chapter 10. The [Minoan sphere guide](/minoan-sphere-light-puzzle-guide/) covers each power.

In a mixed room, the order is: parry the person who has already seen you, then handle the light gate or distant target. Do not swap tools after a swing has started; that is how a planning mistake gets blamed on the parry window. If a room is gated by light, treat it as a puzzle beat, not a damage race. Heritage Pack cosmetics do not add a new combat verb.

## Focus Attack

Focus unlocks through the story, not with skill points. The gauge fills as you attack, parry, and dodge; when it is full, Sophia sweeps everything around her. Walkthrough coverage notes it is also the escape from a Petrified grab in the late chapters, so do not spend it the moment it fills. Theseus and Ariadne have their own Focus Attacks in the memory sections, and Sophia's purchases do not carry over to them. If you want Focus on a different input, see the [Focus Attack rebind guide](/resonance-focus-prism-input-planning/).

## Combat achievements and how common they are

Several Steam achievements are pure combat checks. Rates were read from the Steam global achievement page and drift over time:

| Achievement | Requirement | Steam rate |
|---|---|---|
| Returning the favor | Kill an Archer with a throwing knife | 50.5% |
| Sharp enough for you? | Kill 25 enemies with a critical strike | 43.9% |
| Skilled maneuverer | Stun 3 enemies at once with Echo's Return | 21.5% |
| Untouchable | Parry 6 attacks in a row | 20.3% |
| Inherited brutality | Kill 4 enemies at once with a Focus attack | 4.8% |

Those rates show the combat challenges are optional pressure, not the story floor. Untouchable is easiest against a single opponent with a predictable string; the [missable achievements guide](/missable-achievements-guide/) and the [Inherited Brutality planner](/resonance-inherited-brutality-adaptable/) cover the harder ones.
