## Start with the exact Chapter IX symptom
Resonance's October 6 bug-fix update addressed a Chapter IX problem in which Sophia might be unable to crouch in a petrified area after backtracking in the palace. This is a specific combination of chapter, area, and action. If your current obstacle matches it, begin with the updated game and the current objective rather than assuming you missed a collectible, lost an ability, or need to replay every earlier chapter. The fix concerns traversal state after returning through an area.

This guide gives you a careful way to resume the route and preserve useful progress information. It is not a fabricated map with invented coordinates or a promise that every blocked passage in the palace has the same cause. Read the game's active objective and use the route you have actually unlocked. If you are in another chapter or cannot interact with a different object, identify that situation first; several separate issues were addressed in the same update, and their recovery checks should remain separate too.

## Prepare the save before revisiting the palace
Confirm that the update has finished installing, then load the save in which you encountered the problem. Note your chapter, the current objective, and where you stand relative to the petrified area. If your platform and save setup permit a backup, preserve one before experimenting with an older checkpoint. A copy of the affected state can be useful if the problem persists. It also protects you from replacing the only save that contains the progress you wanted to recover.

Avoid starting several unrelated changes at once. Rebinding movement, loading a distant chapter, changing difficulty, and returning to the palace in the same experiment makes it difficult to understand what actually changed. Begin with the simplest question: can Sophia crouch at the affected location on the updated build? If the answer is yes, continue the intended route and check the next objective. You do not need to repeat an entire evening of exploration just to confirm that one traversal action is available again.

## Approach the passage with a controlled route
Follow the current route back toward the location where crouching failed. As you approach, observe whether the game presents an interaction prompt, permits free movement, or appears to be waiting for a scripted action. Use the current controls and do not assume a prompt from an old video still applies to your input device. If another obstacle blocks the approach, investigate it as its own problem rather than treating every difficulty in the chapter as part of the historical crouch bug.

At the relevant passage, make one deliberate crouch attempt and observe the result. If Sophia enters the posture and can continue, move through normally and allow any objective or checkpoint update to complete. If the action still fails, record whether crouching works elsewhere nearby. That distinction helps separate a local traversal state from an input issue affecting the whole game. Repeatedly pressing several movement and interaction buttons at once produces less useful evidence than a clear account of what one action did in one place.

## Preserve exploration decisions without inventing progress
Backtracking often combines more than one intention: following the story, checking an earlier route, and looking for something you missed. Decide which of those you are doing before taking another detour. If the story objective is the immediate goal, finish that step before conducting a broad collectible sweep. This is a route-planning recommendation rather than a claim that a particular collectible becomes unavailable at a specific moment. The current game and journal should guide decisions about what you can reach.

When you find something useful during a return trip, note the location and the checkpoint context. Do not assume an item remains recorded after a death or reload unless your current save demonstrates it. If you later return to confirm the pickup, compare the relevant collection or journal screen instead of relying only on whether an object appears visually present. The important habit is to verify progress through the game's own record, especially when a route already involved a reload to investigate a traversal problem.

## Keep other chapter fixes on their own branches
Chapter III's Leni's lens interaction was separately named in the October update. If your problem is an unresponsive lens there, a palace crouch test will not diagnose it. Start with the lens's current objective and prompt on the updated build. Chapter XIII also received a fix for a scenario in which progression could not be saved after an old corrupted squeeze-through state. That description concerns saving and a particular traversal history, so record whether a save or checkpoint actually persists when investigating it.

Chapter XIV had another distinct correction: the Book could become unusable after the credits. A post-credits interface problem should be described with that timing rather than folded into a general claim that all backtracking is broken. The patch also fixed multiple object-interaction blocks and collision issues. These broader entries are useful context, but they do not establish a guaranteed solution for every location. A report with a chapter, objective, action, and observed outcome remains more useful than one that simply lists every historical bug in the game.

## Resume the story after a successful check
If the Chapter IX passage works, stop troubleshooting and return to your intended route. Keep an eye on the next normal progression update, then continue playing. Repeatedly loading and reloading a repaired section can waste the time you meant to spend exploring. Make one brief note of the build and successful action if you want a record, then let the current game guide the next decision. A recovered traversal state is a chance to regain momentum rather than a reason to redesign the entire playthrough.

If the issue persists, preserve the affected save and record a concise reproduction: Chapter IX, palace backtracking, the petrified area, whether crouch works elsewhere, and what happened after updating. Include your platform and input device when reporting it. Do not delete all progress in an attempt to make a report sound more decisive.
