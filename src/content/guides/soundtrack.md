**Olivier Derivière** scores *Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy*, as he did *Innocence* and *Requiem* — but he is deliberately not reusing the sound. Everything on this page comes from **DEVBLOG #4, "The Melody Resonates"**, the interview Focus Entertainment and Asobo published to the official Steam news hub on **29 July 2026**.

## The brief: mystical, personal, and grounded at once

Derivière describes long sessions with Creative Director **David Dedeine** and Audio Director **Aurélien Piters** before writing anything, working out an approach for an episode that departs from the earlier games in mythology, storytelling and gameplay alike.

What he came out of those discussions wanting was, in his words, something he had never done before: **mystical yet personal**, emotional but full of action, and still anchored in both the real world and the game's fantasy. His stated goal was to honour the *A Plague Tale* DNA **without repeating himself** — new sounds, new instruments, and what he calls a new signature, so that Resonance "gets its own existence."

The emotional target is specific, and it is Sophia. He frames her as a strong warrior who is broken inside, lost and looking for answers — determined, but facing challenges that can overwhelm that confidence. **The balance between her strength and her fragility is the stated main focus of the score.**

## Why the cello had to change

**Eric Maria Couturier's** cello is part of the series' musical identity, and Derivière is explicit that the instrument stays part of the signature. For Resonance, though, he wanted the same emotional role delivered in "a more Ottoman/Greek way."

That search led him to **Evgenios Voulgaris**, one of the most established players of the **Yayli Tambur** — which the devblog describes as an ancestor of the cello — and the **rebab**. Derivière says Voulgaris is "actually everywhere in the game." Recording happened in both directions: Derivière travelled to **Patras, Greece**, and Voulgaris came to **Paris**, on top of remote sessions.

## The choir: Vasiliki Anastasiou and Amalgamation

To "capture the spirit of Sophia," Derivière went looking for a solo singer and found **Vasiliki Anastasiou** and her choir **Amalgamation**, based in **Cyprus**. Dedeine signed off enthusiastically; Derivière says the voices could "inspire mysticism, empowerment, all sorts of emotions."

The songwriting workflow is the most concrete detail in the interview, and it took **over a year**:

1. Anastasiou travelled to **Paris** for a couple of days to build the workflow with Derivière.
2. He wrote the **lyrics in French**.
3. She translated them into **Cypriot Greek**.
4. She read and recorded them so he could hear the accents.
5. Only then did he write the **final music** around those readings.

Derivière credits her, and her longtime friend **Giorgos Kalogirou** — who also sings in the game — for the result, and says having the lyrics in her own language "made a huge difference."

The recording itself was **two straight days in Cyprus** with Derivière's engineer, roughly **nine hours of singing per day**, with the choir arriving fully prepared. He notes that Anastasiou was **seven months pregnant** through those sessions, and has **dedicated the game's whole score to her daughter, Cleo**.

## What he wanted the voices to do

Derivière's stated intent for the choir is thematic rather than atmospheric: a choir like this carries "sonorities that have echoed through thousands of years," and the game's title is the point — the music is meant to **resonate with our collective past as humans**. He adds that in the finished game the voices "always appear for a good reason," and expects players to remember them.

## Confirmed vs not confirmed

- **Confirmed:** Olivier Derivière composes; Evgenios Voulgaris plays Yayli Tambur and rebab; Vasiliki Anastasiou and the Amalgamation choir recorded in Cyprus; Giorgos Kalogirou sings; lyrics written in French and translated to Cypriot Greek; Eric Maria Couturier's cello remains part of the series signature; David Dedeine (Creative Director) and Aurélien Piters (Audio Director) shaped the approach.
- **Not confirmed:** the track list, the total runtime, whether a standalone OST release or vinyl is planned, and which scenes the choir pieces score. The devblog does not name a single track.
