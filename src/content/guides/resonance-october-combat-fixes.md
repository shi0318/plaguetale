The bug-fix update for **Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy** is a combat patch as much as a technical one. Asobo's notes list enemies who could survive a push off an edge, enemies who stopped reacting after a restart, a "New skill available" popup that could fail to appear after Resonance points, and a Chapter III lens interaction with Leni that could not be triggered. If a fight from the first week still feels wrong, install this update before you rewrite your parry timing.

The game has been out since **August 27, 2026**. On **October 20, 2026** Steam App **2713000** was **$49.99** with no discount, and reviews were Very Positive: **5,676** positive out of **6,117**. That score is not a reason to skip a patch. A Very Positive game can still have a guard who ignores you after a reload. The notes say they fixed that case.

## What changed in a fight

Four lines in the combat and gameplay sections are the ones to retest:

- Enemies who were pushed off an edge could survive. That is marked fixed. A shove that used to be a coin flip should now be a kill if they actually leave the ledge.
- Enemies could stop reacting to your presence or your attacks after a restart. That is marked fixed. If a guard stood still in September, do not build a stealth route around him until you have reloaded on this patch.
- The "New skill available" popup could fail to show after you gained Resonance points from a fight. The points may still have been there. The popup was the bug. Open the skill menu after a fight instead of waiting for a banner that this patch says it restored.
- Numerous smaller collision fixes in specific fights. A swing that used to pass through a shoulder may now connect. That is a buff to the hit, not a nerf to you, unless you were relying on the whiff.

None of these lines publish a new damage number. The parry is still the parry. What changed is whether the enemy is allowed to ignore the result. [Turn, then parry](/how-to-parry-a-flank/) is still the flank rule. This page is the reason a flank from August might not reproduce.

## Chapter bugs that look like combat bugs

The design and gameplay sections name rooms, not just "stability."

- Chapter IX: you might not have been able to crouch in a petrified area after backtracking in the palace. If a stealth line failed because the crouch would not take, that was this bug. Retest the crouch before you call the room a fight.
- Chapter XIV: the book was not usable after the credits. That is a post-game tool bug, not a combat nerf.
- Chapter XIII: a corrupted squeeze-through could block saving progression. If a death in that chapter ate more than the fight, this is the line that matches.
- Chapter III: Leni's lens interaction could fail to trigger. A puzzle you "solved" by leaving the room may have been this trigger, not a missing item.
- Joystick swap preference was not saved after a relaunch. If your hands felt wrong after a reboot, check the binding before you blame the enemy.
- The Falchion could disappear from inventory after installing the DLC. If the sword is gone, this is the bug to check before you assume you sold it.

NPC behavior was also improved in multiple chapters, and Nemesis navigation was improved. A pursuer who took a new line is not automatically a new attack pattern. Walk the old route once on the new patch and see whether the navigation fix is the whole difference.

## Input fixes that belong in the same install

Three control bugs are easy to misread as difficulty:

- Book navigation inputs could not be modified.
- The Focus Attack input could not be changed in the control settings.
- The Prism unlock input could not be changed.

If you rebound the parry and the Focus Attack stayed on the old key, you were not bad at the fight. You were on a menu the patch says was broken. Rebind Focus Attack after the update, then fight one guard. Do not rebind five things and then judge the patch.

AMD machines got a separate line: antilag not working when frame generation was disabled. 21:9 support was fixed. Stutter when approaching a new Nemesis area was reduced. Those are performance lines. A stutter at a Nemesis door can feel like the enemy teleported. If the door is smooth on this patch, the "teleport" may have been the hitch the notes named.

## What to retest, in order

1. Confirm the bug-fix update is the build you are on. The October 6 notes are the long list. Playing a launch install in late October and calling a guard immortal is a version mistake.
2. Shove one enemy off an edge you already know. If they die, the survival bug is gone in that spot.
3. Restart the game, load a fight, and see whether the guard reacts. The non-reaction bug was specifically after a restart.
4. Finish a fight that grants Resonance points and open the skill menu even if the popup appears. Trust the menu.
5. If the Falchion is missing after a DLC install, check the inventory bug before you backtrack for an hour.
6. Rebind Focus Attack if you need it on another button. Then parry one flank.

Steam lists **36** achievements. On October 20, **ACH_01** was at **92.9%** and the thin end of the saved sample included **ACH_22** at **9.2%**, **ACH_25** at **9.3%**, and **ACH_19** at **9.6%**. A combat fix does not hand you the rare pops. It stops a restart from turning a living guard into scenery, which is how a 9% achievement stays at 9% for the wrong reason.

Minimum spec on the store page is still serious: Windows 10 or 11, a Ryzen 3 3100 or i3-12100, **16 GB** of RAM, a 6 GB RX 5500 XT or GTX 1060, **75 GB** on an SSD, and a DX12.1 GPU, targeting 30 frames at 1080p on Low. Recommended is a Ryzen 5 5600X or i5-12400F and an 8 GB RX 6700 XT or RTX 3060 Ti. The stutter fix does not lower that bar. If you are under it, the Nemesis hitch may still be the machine.

Very Positive at **5,676 / 6,117** means most players already liked the island. The patch is for the fights that lied. Install it, retest the edge and the restart, and only then decide a guard is still wrong. The **$49.99** price did not drop because the popup was fixed. The fight did.
