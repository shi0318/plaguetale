## Three achievements built on the environment, not the sword
Most of Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy's visible combat achievements reward how you use the space around Sophia rather than how hard she hits. Three of them form a natural group: one asks you to push enemies off drops, one asks you to stun them with throwables, and one asks for a single throwing-knife kill on an archer. All three are counters or one-off tricks that you can work on during an ordinary playthrough, and none needs a special difficulty or a perfect fight.

Here is where they stand on Valve's public achievement page, checked on November 4, 2026:

- **Returning the favor**: "Kill an Archer with a throwing knife", unlocked by **66.7%** of players.
- **No holds barred**: "Stun 20 enemies with throwables", **39.0%**.
- **Overboard**: "Kill 20 enemies by pushing them down a drop", **33.0%**.

For context, **A new beginning**, the completion achievement, is now at 61.0%. That means most players who reached the end picked up Returning the favor along the way, but a large share finished the story without 20 throwable stuns or 20 ledge kills. Both counters climbed a long way since this site's [achievements guide](/achievements-guide/) first logged them on September 1, when No holds barred stood at 21.9% and Overboard at 20.1%. They are no longer rare, but they are the visible combat achievements most likely to still be missing after the credits.

## Overboard: how ledge kills work
Overboard counts enemies that die by being pushed down a drop. It is cumulative, so you do not need 20 in one fight; every ledge kill across the save adds to it. The community "Achievements and Collectables" guide on Steam by .• ellovu •. (posted August 31, updated September 7) puts it simply: there are multiple fights where you can push enemies off cliffs, edges, or anywhere with a drop, and you can lead them to an edge and kick them off.

The planning lesson is to read each arena before you draw a sword. Resonance's island is full of ruined terraces, broken bridges, cliffside paths and raised platforms, and plenty of fights happen next to one. When you spot a drop, move so that the enemy ends up between Sophia and the edge rather than the other way round. That positioning is the whole trick. If the enemy is standing with open ground behind them, the push only buys space; if there is a drop behind them, the push ends the fight for that enemy and adds one to your count.

Make the edge part of your plan from the first second of the fight rather than an afterthought once the group is nearly dead. The fewer enemies you finish with the sword, the more chances you have to send someone over the drop instead, and every enemy removed that way also makes the rest of the encounter smaller and easier to read.

## The October 6 patch changed ledge kills
If you tried for Overboard early and felt that some pushes did not count, you may have been right. The official October 6 bug-fix update on Steam lists, under Combat, "Fixed an issue where enemies could survive after getting pushed off the edge." An enemy that survived the fall would not have counted as a kill, so pushes that failed before the patch could have left your counter lower than you expected.

The same update also lists "Numerous minor fixes to collisions in specific fights" and "Fixed numerous collision issues." Collision affects whether a body actually goes over a ledge or catches on the geometry, so if a particular edge behaved strangely before October 6, retry it on the current build before assuming it cannot be used. The [ledge-shove retest guide](/resonance-ledge-shove-retest/) covers a controlled way to check the fix in one encounter, and the [October combat bug-fix guide](/resonance-october-combat-bugfix-guide/) collects the other combat changes from that update. Steam lists no newer patch for the game since October 6, so that build is still the one to test against.

## No holds barred: stunning with throwables
No holds barred needs 20 stuns from throwables, again counted across the save. The community guide calls it easy and adds one important detail: throw your bottles at enemies without shields, because the shielded ones can block them. A blocked throw is wasted, both for the fight and for the counter, so pick unshielded targets and save the shield-bearers for your sword.

Throwables are pickups you find during fights, which is why many players stall on this counter. They grab a bottle, use it whenever it happens to be in hand, and never think about the total. Two habits fix that. First, treat every throwable you see during an encounter as achievement progress and throw it at a valid target before the fight ends. Second, consider the **Fated Cast** skill. The same community guide recommends spending Resonance Points on it because it lets you hold one throwable during a fight, and the [skills unlock order guide](/plague-tale-legacy-skills-unlock-order/) describes it as storing a picked-up throwable on your belt to use later. That turns a random pickup into something you can aim at the right enemy at the right moment.

Stuns also set up the next problem. A stunned enemy is an opening, so follow up with a hit or, if you are standing near an edge, a push. Stunning an enemy beside a drop and then sending them over it advances No holds barred and Overboard in the same exchange.

## Returning the favor: one knife, one archer
Returning the favor needs a single throwing-knife kill on an archer. According to the community guide, a throwing knife does enough damage to kill an archer in one hit, so there is no need to soften the target first. The same author suggests Fated Cast here too: pick up a throwing knife, keep it on your belt, and throw it at an archer in the same fight.

Archers show up early: the same community guide's Chapter II notes mention learning to grapple an archer off its position, so you will have chances long before the late game. When a fight includes an archer and you have a knife in hand, resist using it on whatever is closest. Two-thirds of players already have this one, so if you are still missing it, the likely reason is that the knife was thrown at someone else.

## Fold all three into normal play
None of these achievements needs a dedicated session. The efficient approach is to change a few defaults in every fight:

1. Scan the arena for drops before engaging, and fight with your back to solid ground.
2. Throw every throwable you pick up at an unshielded enemy, and keep one on your belt once Fated Cast is unlocked.
3. If an archer is present, reserve your throwing knife for it.
4. Use stuns as the setup for a push whenever an edge is close.

If you still need a few after the credits, [Story Select](/how-to-use-story-select/) lets you return to chapters with plenty of cliffside fights rather than replaying the whole game. The same principle, using enemies and space against each other, also drives **Cannon fodder** (luring a raging Brute into killing a soldier, now at 51.2%) and **Skilled maneuverer** (stunning three enemies at once with Echo's Return, now at 34.3%). Those have their own walkthroughs in the [Cannon fodder guide](/plague-tale-legacy-cannon-fodder-achievement/) and the [Echo's Return guide](/plague-tale-legacy-echos-return/). For a broader look at how Sophia's sling, melee and pickups fit together, see [sling and melee](/plague-tale-legacy-sling-and-melee/).
