Resonance runs across **two eras and three broad settings**, and Asobo's art director and narrative team laid out the real-world references for all of them in **DEVBLOG #3**. This page collects what is confirmed about the places you travel through. It is not a route guide — chapter-by-chapter routing belongs on the [walkthrough hub](/walkthrough/) when each route has been directly documented.

## The two time periods

- **Early 14th century** — Sophia's era, roughly 15 years before *A Plague Tale: Requiem*.
- **The Minoan civilisation, around 2000 BC** — the ancient half, some **2,000 years earlier**.

Asobo call the Minoan period one that "as far as we know, has never really been touched" in games. That is not marketing puff so much as a practical problem they had to solve, and it shapes everything below.

## The raiders' camp in the Venice lagoon

The adventure opens in a **plunderers' camp in the lagoons near Venice** — described as rough and poor, but still full of life. Two things about how it was made are worth knowing:

- It was built out from the **Red City Arena in Requiem**, with worn fabrics and hanging cloth added to make it feel warmer and more lived-in.
- Because 14th-century references for that kind of settlement barely exist, the team looked at **working-class districts in Africa and Asia**, particularly the wooden structures common at the time.

This is also where DEVBLOG #1 places Sophia's childhood, in a camp led by her father **Alec**.

## Venice and the carnival

Venice itself is "still largely preserved from that era," so the architecture was the straightforward part. The hard part was the **carnival**: huge crowds, and a city that had to feel real rather than idealised.

A carnival sequence with dense crowds is a notable technical commitment for this series, which has historically favoured tight, low-population set pieces punctuated by rat swarms.

## The island

The largest chunk of the game happens on **the legendary island**, hidden somewhere in the Mediterranean. The stated design worry was **repetition**, and the answer was to pull references from across Greece rather than settling on one look:

| Reference | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| The Cyclades | White limestone cliffs, turquoise water, pine — the baseline look |
| Santorini, Stromboli | Volcanic landscapes, darker rock, an unsettling mood in some levels |
| Thiorichio | More colourful rock formations |
| Preveli Beach | Varied vegetation |
| **Knossos, Akrotiri** | The Minoan architecture itself |

The compromise they describe is honest about its own invention: they kept core Minoan elements — **columns and friezes** — then blended in **later Greek and Roman influences** so the large-scale arenas would read as coherent, because structures at that scale did not exist in the period. Broader references were *Assassin's Creed Odyssey* for ancient Greece, and *Gladiator* and *Troy* for a more human take on the era.

The result, in their words, is a tension between the **restrained, colourful Minoan look** and **massive, almost excessive arenas** — plus familiar Mediterranean scenery set against ominous volcanic terrain.

## Artifacts as storytelling

Rather than narrating the island's history, Asobo decided to let players **live it** — largely through the artifacts Sophia finds. These are drawn from real Minoan objects and written as pieces of a puzzle she is assembling. Two examples they give:

- An **orrery** — why is a device for predicting the movement of the stars on this island?
- **Seal stones** depicting people playing with a bull before killing it — a sport, a symbolic trial, or a sacrifice?

Combined with the **notebook** the store page says guides her, this is where the game's optional content most likely lives. We track that on the [collectibles page](/collectibles/).

## A note on historical accuracy

Asobo draw a distinction worth repeating: **sense of historicity** (what keeps you believing you are in the 14th century) versus **factual historicity** (what is documented). A medieval history associate professor who worked on Requiem advised them again here.

Where the two conflicted, they chose immersion — their example being that both "By cock's bones!" and "Fie on this world!" are historically attested and would make a player laugh at exactly the wrong moment.

## Confirmed vs not confirmed

- **Confirmed:** the lagoon camp, Venice with a carnival sequence, and the Minoan island; the two eras; artifacts including an orrery and bull-leaping seal stones.
- **Not confirmed:** chapter count, chapter order, how much of the island is open, and whether the Venice sections are early-game only.
